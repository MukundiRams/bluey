# Personal Loans

## How does a Standard Bank personal loan work?

A personal loan is repaid through monthly instalments over an agreed period. The interest rate and repayment period depend on the amount borrowed and the repayment timeframe.

Standard Bank assesses each application individually. The assessment considers factors including:

- Credit score and creditworthiness
- Existing debt and repayment history
- Income and affordability
- Requirements under the National Credit Act (NCA)

Standard Bank states that interest rates are personalised in accordance with NCA requirements and that a stronger credit profile may result in a better interest rate.

## What should I consider before taking a personal loan?

Consider whether the loan is appropriate for your needs and financial circumstances.

The interest rate and repayment period affect the total cost of borrowing. A longer repayment period may reduce the monthly instalment but can increase the total amount paid over the life of the loan.

Standard Bank recommends using its personal-loan calculators to estimate qualification, monthly repayments and the effect of changes to the term, interest rate, missed payments and early payments.

## How do I apply for a personal loan?

You can apply through:

- **Online Banking**
- **Standard Bank Banking App**
- **Cellphone Banking:** dial `*120*5626`
- **Telephone:** call `0860 123 000` and select option 5
- **A Standard Bank branch**

## Is a Standard Bank personal loan insured?

Yes. Standard Bank states that its personal loans include **credit life insurance**.

## How long does it take to receive the loan funds?

The stated timelines are:

| Applicant / application method | Stated funding time |
|---|---|
| Existing Standard Bank customer applying through Online Banking or the Banking App | Immediately*, subject to terms and conditions |
| Existing Standard Bank customer applying in a branch | 1–2 working days |
| Existing Standard Bank customer using the website's "Call Me Back" option | 1–2 working days |
| Non-Standard Bank customer | 2–3 working days, subject to outstanding verification conditions such as income confirmation |

`*` Standard Bank states that terms and conditions apply.

## Can I increase my credit limit?

Yes, Standard Bank offers credit-limit increases on its **Revolving Loan** and **Overdraft** products.

You can request an increase through:

- Banking App
- Online Banking
- Standard Bank website
- A Standard Bank branch

Credit-limit increases are subject to affordability and good credit standing.

## How and when do I make loan repayments?

During the loan process, Standard Bank arranges a debit order to collect repayments.

The debit-order date can be aligned with your salary date or another preferred date.

You can also make additional payments into the loan account, which can help reduce the outstanding loan balance.

## What is the minimum income required to qualify?

Standard Bank currently states:

- **Term Loan:** minimum income of **R3,000 per month**
- **Revolving Loan / Overdraft:** minimum income of **R8,000 per month**

Meeting the income threshold does not by itself guarantee approval; applications are assessed individually.

## How long will I wait for feedback after applying?

Standard Bank states that customers applying through Online Banking, the Banking App, the website, in a branch or through Cellphone Banking will receive an immediate indication* of whether the application has been approved or declined.

`*` Subject to the applicable conditions and verification requirements.

## Can I get a settlement letter after paying my loan?

Yes. You can request a settlement letter through:

- Your nearest Standard Bank branch
- Your banker
- Voice Branch

## Where can I view my remaining loan balance?

Your loan balance can be viewed through:

- Online Banking
- Banking App
- A Standard Bank branch
- Voice Branch

## My loan account has been handed over to lawyers. Who do I contact?

If your account has been handed over to lawyers, Standard Bank says you can:

- Visit your nearest branch, or
- Call **0860 123 000**

## What happens when my loan has been paid off?

For a **Term Loan**, Standard Bank states that the account automatically closes once it has been paid up or settled.

A **Revolving Credit Plan** or **Overdraft** can be closed:

- In a branch
- Through your banker
- By contacting Standard Bank on **0860 123 000**

# Credit Cards

## What is the difference between my available balance and latest balance?

Your **available balance** is the amount currently available for you to spend.

Your **latest balance** includes outstanding authorisations reserved by merchants.

For example, your latest credit-card balance may show R1,500 while your available balance is R1,000. The R500 difference may represent one or more outstanding authorisations reserved for a merchant.

## What is an outstanding authorisation?

When you use your credit card for a purchase, the purchase amount is set aside for payment to the merchant.

That amount is therefore temporarily unavailable for further spending.

When the merchant processes ("banks") the transaction, the transaction is settled and the authorisation falls away.

## What is the difference between a finance charge and a cash finance charge?

A **finance charge** is interest calculated on your outstanding credit-card balance.

A **cash finance charge** is interest calculated on a transaction treated as a cash transaction.

## Why am I charged a cash finance charge during the 55-day interest-free period?

The 55-day interest-free period applies to **purchase transactions**, including purchases made:

- In person through a card machine
- Through e-commerce
- Through m-commerce

Cash transactions are different: Standard Bank states that they attract interest from the time the transaction is performed.

## Which transactions are treated as cash transactions?

Standard Bank lists the following as cash transactions that contribute to the cash finance charge:

- Cash advances / withdrawals
- Electronic transfers and account payments
- Purchases of travellers' cheques
- Petrol purchases
- Online gambling
- Casino-chip purchases
- Foreign-exchange purchases

## How does the 55-day interest-free period work?

The credit card provides up to **55 days** to repay purchase transactions without interest, provided the **full closing balance shown on the statement is paid by the payment due date**.

The cycle works as follows:

1. The statement covers a 30-day period.
2. You then have 25 additional days to settle the closing balance in full.
3. If the full closing balance is paid by the due date, qualifying purchase transactions do not attract interest.
4. If the balance is not paid in full, interest can accrue.

### Example

If transactions occur from **1 July to 31 July**, the example given by Standard Bank has a payment due date of **25 August** for the full outstanding balance.

The 55-day interest-free period **does not apply to cash transactions**.

## What different types of interest can be charged on a credit card?

Standard Bank identifies three categories:

1. **Purchase / straight-facility transactions**
2. **Cash transactions**
3. **Budget transactions**

Budget transactions are purchase transactions repaid over an agreed period such as:

- 3 months
- 6 months
- 12 months
- 24 months
- 36 months

If you have transactions in multiple categories and do not settle the card in full within the applicable interest-free period, you can have different interest charges for those categories.

## How are repayments allocated when I have straight, budget and cash transactions?

Standard Bank states that repayments are allocated in this order:

1. **Straight-facility purchase transactions**
2. **Budget-facility purchase transactions**
3. **Cash-finance transactions**

### Example

Suppose your outstanding amounts are:

- R100 straight-facility purchase
- R200 budget-facility purchase
- R50 cash transaction

Your total outstanding amount is R350.

If you pay the full R350, the repayment is allocated:

1. R100 to the straight facility
2. R200 to the budget facility
3. R50 to the cash facility

If you pay only R100, the entire R100 is allocated to the straight facility. The remaining R200 budget balance and R50 cash balance remain outstanding and continue to attract applicable interest.

## What is the international transaction fee?

The **international transaction fee** is a fee charged for facilitating an international transaction made with a Standard Bank credit card.

Standard Bank states that the fee applies when you purchase from a service provider outside South Africa.

## What percentage is the international transaction fee?

Standard Bank's FAQ currently states an international transaction fee of **2.75% of the Rand value of the transaction**.

For example, if the Rand value of an international purchase is R100, the stated fee would be R2.75.

The FAQ gives examples including:

- Using the card at a merchant while travelling outside South Africa
- Making an online purchase in South Africa from an international merchant, such as an international subscription service

## Why does my credit-card balance and credit limit show as zero?

If both your credit-card balance and credit limit temporarily show as zero, Standard Bank says this can occur while its systems are undergoing scheduled updates.

During these periods:

- Credit-card transactions may temporarily be unavailable for viewing in the Banking App or Online Banking.
- Your funds remain secure and available.
- Your card can continue to be used for purchases at merchants, including in-person and e-commerce transactions.
